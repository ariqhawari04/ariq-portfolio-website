The question

How can raw match results become a useful tool for exploring team performance, season progression, and historical rivalries?

My contribution

I built and continue to develop the application, integrating historical datasets and current-season API data, structuring the database, implementing analytical views, and adding feedback and engagement tracking.

The product

League Overview, Team Explorer, Team Comparison, and Head-to-Head Explorer cover 2015/16–2026/27. Visitors can explore standings, home and away performance, points progression, and the matches behind club records.

What this demonstrates

Data integration, reusable analytical logic, automated workflows, interactive communication, and ongoing product iteration.

From source to application

OpenFootball + football-data.org→Python validation→PostgreSQL→Streamlit + Plotly
  • Data quality: normalise club names, detect duplicate fixtures, verify match coverage, and incorporate known historical points deductions.
  • Automation: schedule current-season ingestion four times daily, validate incoming results, and upsert new or changed records.
  • Product learning: collect optional ratings and feedback, with session and feature-view reporting in a protected administration dashboard.

Scope: descriptive and comparative analysis. Updates are periodic, not real-time. The app does not yet include player-level data, xG, or predictive models, and not all official standings tie-breakers are implemented. Usage counts represent recorded sessions, not unique people.

Read the case study

The problem

Football clubs face commercial pressure while supporters face rising ticket costs. My research explored how pricing decisions could account for both revenue and affordability.

Data & approach

I combined attendance and stadium data, team rankings and squad valuations, weather, and travel distances. Engineered features captured occupancy history, match context, rivalry, and timing.

Forecast demandApply affordability constraintsMap Pareto trade-offs

Analytical findings

The Random Forest model assigned substantial predictive importance to stadium capacity and past occupancy, alongside team-quality and contextual price interactions. These describe model behaviour and associations; they do not establish causal effects.

Limitations

Blended historical price proxies and limited seat, inventory, and purchase-timing data constrain interpretation. The analysis is oriented toward broader match and season patterns, rather than an operational real-time pricing system. Results may not generalise beyond Premier League conditions.

Academic research and simulation. The portfolio does not claim that the pricing framework was deployed by a club or generated measured commercial uplift.

Customer segmentation with RFM & K-MeansCustomer analytics · Python · scikit-learn · Public-data reconstruction

Context & contribution

Inspired by a customer-segmentation proof of concept I developed at Telkom Indonesia. This public reconstruction uses 25,000+ retail transaction logs to demonstrate the analytical workflow.

Approach

Clean missing customer IDs and invalid transactions; aggregate recency, frequency, and monetary value; apply log transformation and standardisation; then explore three K-Means clusters using the elbow method and a PCA projection.

Proposed activation

Use Champions, Potential Loyalists, and At-Risk / Hibernating profiles to inform differentiated retention and reactivation hypotheses.

PCA is a visual diagnostic, not proof of cluster validity. Stability checks, additional validation metrics, and campaign testing would be needed before claiming business impact.

Promotion performance & experimental analysisPersonal project · Python · ANOVA · Tukey HSD

The business question

How do weekly sales differ across three promotional campaigns, and what does the evidence support before a broader rollout?

Unit of analysis

The dataset contains 548 weekly observations from 137 stores. I aggregated the four observations per store to store-level averages to avoid treating repeated observations as independent samples.

Findings & interpretation

The reported one-way ANOVA result (p = 0.0037) indicates evidence of a difference in mean sales across campaigns. Tukey HSD comparisons found lower sales for Promotion 2 than Promotions 1 and 3. The comparison between Promotions 1 and 3 was not statistically significant (p = 0.6862); this does not establish equivalence.

Recommendation

Investigate the weaker performance of Promotion 2 and compare campaign costs, margins, and uncertainty before choosing a rollout. Report effect estimates and confidence intervals alongside significance tests.

These are analytical findings from a portfolio dataset, not a realised ROI result. The small-market subgroup contains only 15 stores, so subgroup findings require particular caution.
