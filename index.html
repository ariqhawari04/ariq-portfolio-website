Master's Dissertation
AI-driven dynamic ticket pricing optimization for the English Premier League
Python · Multi-objective optimization · Predictive modelling · Pareto frontier analysis

The Core Tension

While Premier League clubs depend heavily on rising matchday revenues to remain competitive, regular price increases have sparked widespread fan protests over affordability. This tension underscores a systemic market failure: clubs are deep-rooted community assets, yet commercial pressures often push owners to prioritize short-term revenue maximization over long-term fan accessibility.

The Data & Feature Engineering

To model dynamic demand accurately, I consolidated and engineered a comprehensive multi-source dataset spanning several structural dimensions:

  • Match & Team Metrics (FBref & PL Handbook): Historical attendance, stadium capacity, and previous season rankings.
  • Squad Valuation (Transfermarkt): Home and away top-5 player market values used as proxies for team quality and drawing power.
  • External Variables (Open-Meteo API & Google Maps): Historical matchday weather parameters and calculated distances between stadiums (km).
  • Engineered Contextual Features: Derived metrics including previous fixture fill percentages, empty seat counts, and explicit rivalry/big-match indicators.

ML Insights & Feature Importance

Rather than relying on rigid econometric dummies, the optimized machine learning model mapped contextual data patterns, revealing true predictive drivers:

  • Venue Capacity & Occupancy: Stadium capacity acts as the absolute dominant predictor by setting the structural upper bound on attendance. Additionally, past occupancy variables—such as Previous Home Match Fill and Empty Seats—serve as critical demand and fan momentum signals.
  • Team Quality Indicators: Home Team Top-5 Player Market Value emerged as a major demand driver, confirming that stronger home squads are consistently monetized. Conversely, Away Team Quality ranked lower, proving that home-side drawing power dominates the ecosystem while visiting clubs act more as externalities.
  • Price–Demand Interactions: Engineered interaction terms like Price × Previous Fill and Price × Home Ranking ranked highly, demonstrating that local fan price sensitivity is highly fluid and shifts with match context. Furthermore, the Big Six effect is entirely conditional, influencing demand through interaction terms rather than as a standalone feature.
  • Scheduling & Seasonal Effects: Features tracking timing anchors across the sales cycle—specifically Match Week, Season Progress, and Kick-off Hour—along with season dummies, act as significant calendar multipliers for crowd distribution.

How I Solved It (The 3-Stage Pipeline)

I engineered a robust, automated end-to-end pipeline in three stages:

  • Demand Forecasting: Predicting dynamic matchday ticket demand utilizing the optimized Random Forest model to minimize RMSE.
  • Fairness Constraints: Embedding tailored mathematical constraints to lock in affordable price tiers for core supporters.
  • Pareto Frontier Mapping: Modelling optimal trade-off balance points for the Big Six clubs to guide commercial policy.

The Business Value

The framework mathematically demonstrates to club executives that transitioning into an AI-driven, data-backed pricing strategy does not require sacrificing loyal fan communities to unlock sustainable commercial growth.

Research Limitations

To maintain rigorous data transparency, the model's scope is bounded by a few core constraints:

  • Granularity & Variable Coverage: Due to strict data constraints, micro-level features (such as seat location, ticket inventory levels, and exact purchase timing) were excluded, leaning the model toward robust macro season-level predictions rather than fine-grained real-time dynamics.
  • Blended Price Proxies: Ticket price metrics were derived from blended historical season and market data across multiple sources, which smoothed out variance and explains why price itself ranked lower than its interaction terms.
  • EPL Generalisability: The underlying optimization structures reflect uniquely inelastic English Premier League characteristics—such as global demand outstripping supply and high season ticket uptake—and may yield different trade-offs in other football leagues.

Project Overview

A robust data science workflow to segment an online retail dataset containing 25,000+ transaction logs. By combining the RFM framework, mathematical data transformations, and K-Means clustering, this project translates raw data into actionable marketing strategies.

Context & Origin

This project is inspired by a Proof of Concept (PoC) I developed during my time at Telkom Indonesia. Due to data privacy, company data is replaced here with a similarly structured e-commerce dataset from Kaggle to demonstrate the technical framework used in production.

The Business Problem

When treating all customers the same way, marketing campaigns become highly inefficient. To solve this, the marketing team required a data-driven solution to understand typical customer profiles and deliver targeted, cost-effective marketing strategies.

Data Analytics Workflow & Tech Stack

Tech Stack: Python (Pandas, NumPy, Scikit-Learn, Matplotlib, Seaborn)

  • Data Preprocessing: Handled missing Customer IDs and eliminated anomalies (negative quantities/prices).
  • RFM Aggregation: Calculated Recency (days since snapshot), Frequency (unique invoices), and Monetary (total spend) per customer.
  • Feature Transformation: Applied Log Transformation to fix right-skewness and utilized StandardScaler to normalize features for geometric clustering.
  • Optimal K Selection: Determined the ideal cluster count (K=3) using the Elbow Method to balance mathematical variance with business feasibility.
  • Dimensionality Reduction (PCA): Projected the 3D RFM space into 2D via Principal Component Analysis for clear cluster validation.

Personal Project
A/B Testing & Promotion Strategy Optimization
Hypothesis Testing · Python · One-Way ANOVA · Tukey HSD Post-Hoc
Executive Summary & ROI Impact

The Business Dilemma

A fast-food franchise network tested three distinct promotional campaigns across 137 randomized store locations. To mitigate massive capital waste before rollout, corporate management demanded a data-driven mandate: Which campaign drives maximum weekly sales lift, and is the observed performance difference mathematically real or merely market noise?

Core Statistical Finding

Executing a global One-Way ANOVA confirmed highly significant variance differences (p = 0.0037). Post-hoc Tukey HSD evaluation proved that Promotions 1 & 3 are statistically equivalent across all market size segments, while Promotion 2 heavily underperformed.

Strategic Decision

Definitively retire Promotion 2. Since the final sales metrics for Promotions 1 & 3 are statistically identical, executive selection priority must shift entirely to operational cost minimizations to optimize campaign ROI.

Tech Environment: Python (Pandas, SciPy Stats, Statsmodels, Seaborn)

03

Statistical Assumption Auditing

Before trusting the ANOVA model, we must rigorously validate its underlying mathematical assumptions:

Levene's Test (Homoscedasticity)

Why use it? ANOVA requires all groups to have roughly equal variances.
Expected: A p-value > 0.05 (failing to reject the null hypothesis of equal variance).

Result: Yields a robust p = 0.6272. This confirms homoscedastic variances across groups, perfectly fulfilling ANOVA constraints.

Kruskal-Wallis (Distribution Check)

Why use it? The Shapiro-Wilk test flagged a right-skew in the data. We run a non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis check to ensure this non-normality won't distort the ANOVA.
Expected: To show the same significance trend as ANOVA.

Result: Echoes matching global significance (p = 0.00017). This ensures minor data skewness doesn't mathematically invalidate the final inference.

04

Global ANOVA & Tukey HSD Inference

With assumptions validated, we deploy the core statistical tests to identify the true winner.

1. One-Way ANOVA

Why use it? To determine if there is any statistically significant difference in sales across the three promotions overall.
Expected: A p-value < 0.05 to prove a difference exists.

Result: Execution on store-level metrics proves highly significant global performance differences (F = 5.8458, p = 0.0037).

2. Tukey HSD Post-Hoc Test

Why use it? ANOVA only tells us a difference exists, not where. Tukey HSD compares all pairs simultaneously while strictly controlling the family-wise error rate (avoiding false positives).

Result Matrix:

05

Segmented Market-Size Interaction Audits & Advanced Insights

To protect against localized regional anomalies, the dataset was sliced by market sizes to check if structural scale alters the experiment outcome. Slicing the experiment down across the 137 stores reveals a highly consistent strategic pattern across all tiers, backed by rigorous post-hoc testing:

Market Size Segment ANOVA p-value True Statistical Outcome (Tukey HSD)
Large Markets (42 stores) p = 0.0077 Promo 1 & 3 are statistically equivalent (p = 0.941).
Medium Markets (80 stores) p = 0.0001 Promo 1 & 3 remain statistically tied (p = 0.498).
Small Markets (15 stores) p = 0.0032 Promo 1 & 3 are statistically tied (p = 0.950).
Deep-Dive Segment Insights:

1. Large Markets (42 stores): Promotion 3 has the highest nominal average ($77.20K) compared to Promotion 1 ($75.24K), but the two are completely statistically tied with a high p-value of 0.941. Meanwhile, Promotion 2 ($60.32K) is significantly weaker than both campaigns.

2. Medium Markets (80 stores): Promotion 1 ($47.67K) and Promotion 3 ($45.47K) are again statistically tied (p = 0.498), while Promotion 2 ($39.11K) is significantly weaker and underperforms uniformly.

3. Small Markets (15 stores): The exact same pattern holds true—Promotion 1 ($60.16K) and Promotion 3 ($59.51K) are statistically tied (p = 0.950), with Promotion 2 ($50.81K) remaining the weakest link.

Important Statistical Power Caveat: The Small market segment has only 15 stores total (5 / 4 / 6 stores per promotion). While the localized ANOVA is significant, confidence intervals are necessarily wide due to the limited sample size. Therefore, this segment's specific standalone parameters should be treated as suggestive rather than definitive, and revisited as more operational data scales up.
